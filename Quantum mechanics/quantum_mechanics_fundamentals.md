# Two ways to introduce QM
There are two well-known approaches to introduce students to quantum mechanics — the wavefunction approach and the operators approach. The former describes the wavefunction as an object in an infinite dimensional Hilbert space and relies on the techniques in differential equations to solve Shrondinger's Equation and compute observables. The latter introduces quantum states (the wavefunction) and time evolution operations (from Shrondinger's Equation) as vectors and matrices, then applies techniques in linear algebra to process quantum systems. 

The wavefunction approach is the older description of quantum mechanics that can be more mathematically dense than the operator approach. It is suitable for students with a strong mathematical background and interest in the theory and formalism of quantum physics. Textbooks using this approach include [Griffiths's Introduction to Quantum Mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introduction_to_Quantum_Mechanics_(book)).

The operator's approach can be seen in books such as [Quantum Mechanics A Paradigms Approach](https://www.cambridge.org/highereducation/books/quantum-mechanics/8250095A35A2775E21BB609CEF2736C4#overview). Is it a more modern approach that heavily relies on linear algebra for most of its computations. This approach is well-suited for students who wish to study quantum computing and other applications of quantum mechanics.

It is important to reiterate that this distinction between the two approaches is only at the **introductory level**. Students would quickly realize that these two approaches are interchangeable, and any intermediate study in quantum mechanics requires a fluent understanding of both approaches. `TL;DR It is fun to discuss these topics and how to get people into quantum physics. But if you want to do anything more than a surface-level understanding of quantum, I recommend understanding both.`
